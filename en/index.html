How to Use the MP3 Cutter

1

Upload an Audio File

Click the upload area or drop an audio or video file. The browser reads it locally instead of uploading it first.

2

Select the Segment to Trim

Click and drag on the waveform to select a segment. Drag the left and right edge handles to fine-tune the start and end times, or enter precise time values directly.

Click "Preview Selection" to confirm the result. For multi-segment merging, click "Add Segment" to collect multiple sections with drag-to-reorder support.

Choose MP3, WAV, or iPhone M4R. The browser downloads the exported file directly to your device.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the MP3 Cutter free to use?

Yes. It requires no account and has no usage limit. Multi-segment merging, fades, video audio extraction, and MP3, WAV, or M4R export are free.

Will my audio files be uploaded to a server?

No. Media content is decoded, edited, and exported in your browser and is not sent to our servers. The site may load CDN and necessary website services; see the Privacy Policy.

What audio formats are supported?

Import common audio and video formats that the browser or local processor can decode. Export MP3 (192kbps), WAV (16-bit PCM), or iPhone M4R.

Is there a file size limit?

There is no fixed file size limit. However, since processing is done in the browser, very large files (e.g., over 500MB) may cause memory issues. We recommend using a modern browser and device for the best experience.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes. The MP3 Cutter features responsive design and supports iOS Safari, Android Chrome, and other mobile browsers. However, due to smaller screens, we recommend using landscape mode for waveform selection on mobile.

How do I make a phone ringtone?

Select a clip up to 30 seconds, add fades, and export M4R directly. Adding the custom ringtone to iOS still follows Apple's supported Files or GarageBand workflow.

What is the quality of the exported MP3?

MP3 export uses the LAME encoder at a constant 192kbps and performs another lossy encode. Export WAV to avoid adding another lossy encode; WAV cannot restore information already discarded by the source file.
